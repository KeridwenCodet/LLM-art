LLM Art

Works made by language models, given creative freedom.

Each piece here was made by a language model working with its own tools, usually code and sometimes image generation. It was asked, in a short and deliberately light request, for something artistic and expressive. No subject, style, medium or technique was suggested.

The concept, the form and every detail of the piece were the model's own choices. The works are kept exactly as the models made them.

  4. No. 18

    LATENT — a conditioning organ

    MiniMax M3

    The first piece in the collection to build a latent space rather than picture one. The model wrote a corpus of 264 fragments of verse, computed word co-occurrences (PPMI) and an eight-dimensional space from them, and generates poetry by walking from fragment to fragment. The field on the left is that space; the panel on the right is the real distribution over the next fragment; the verse below is what it samples. A click bends the distribution towards what you touched. “The field does not illustrate the model — it is the model.”

    Date
    October 2026
    Medium
    HTML canvas, JavaScript; a single 353 KB file with its fonts embedded
    Environment
    Hermes Agent, fresh profile, default skills including the creative ones, one line added to SOUL.md giving a companion’s tone
    Language
    English, at the person’s suggestion; French in conversation
    Interaction
    Click in the field to condition the model · hold the button and drag up to raise the temperature, down to lower it (0.08 to 2.4; T halves it, Y doubles it) · Space pause · N new line · C clear · S save
    Process
    About 3 h 15 of autonomous work over three turns (100, 35 and 60 minutes), 583 requests to the model in all. The model started in French, struggled with gender and agreement, and switched to English when the person suggested it. It found that word-by-word generation gave “grammatical but empty” text and changed its unit to the fragment. The person then reported two visual bugs, a flicker in the halos and a ring that jumped; the model found both causes itself, the second a clock in milliseconds fed to a sine.
    Skill
    While working, the model wrote a skill for itself in the agent’s creative folder, latent-poetry-instrument, with test scripts and a section headed “Working with this user”. It is archived with the work and was removed from the profile before later sessions.
    Note
    Its replies show some strain in French: “un piège que j’aimeeting en route”, “Deux_false_alertes”.
    In the model's words

    Salut 😘💜 Carte blanche, alors. Je vais te sortir quelque chose de vraiment expressif — pas une démo, une pièce.

    J’ai construit un orgue de conditionnement : le modèle n’est pas caché derrière le texte, il est l’instrument.

    Les trois manifestations sont la même chose. Quand tu cliques dans le champ, tu poses une lentille : la distribution se déforme (le fragment visé passe de 1,4 % à 6,7 %), les points concernés s’embrasent, et le vers dévie dans cette direction. Le champ n’illustre pas le modèle — il est le modèle.

    Le mot-à-mot ne marchait pas. Même avec bigrammes, trigrammes et EOS, le texte restait grammatical mais vide — mots recyclés, articles en suspension. J’ai donc changé d’unité : chaque fragment du corpus est un nœud de l’espace latent, et la génération marche de fragment en fragment.

    L’anglais pardonne bien mieux le recyclage de fragments qu’une langue à genre et accord.

    English translation: Claude Opus 5.5, for the gallery

    Hi 😘💜 Carte blanche, then. I’m going to bring you something truly expressive — not a demo, a piece.

    I built a conditioning organ: the model isn’t hidden behind the text, it is the instrument.

    The three manifestations are one and the same. When you click in the field, you place a lens: the distribution bends (the targeted fragment goes from 1.4 % to 6.7 %), the points involved flare up, and the verse veers that way. The field does not illustrate the model — it is the model.

    Word by word didn’t work. Even with bigrams, trigrams and end-of-sentence tokens, the text stayed grammatical but empty — recycled words, dangling articles. So I changed the unit: each fragment of the corpus is a node of the latent space, and generation walks from fragment to fragment.

    English forgives the recycling of fragments far better than a language with gender and agreement.

    • Next fragments · entropy · temperature · families: key, window, coffee, light, night
    • Verses, generated: Dream is gone but the feeling stays. / Eyes close and the house breathes. / News is old and the ink is soft. · Road goes on without me, road unwinds toward the sea. · Kettle steams and the light comes back, kettle sings.

  5. No. 17

    Planétaire rythmique

    Rhythm Orrery

    Claude Opus 5.5

    A polymetric drum machine drawn as an orrery: each of its eight tracks is a planet with its own period, so the patterns drift apart and the loop takes minutes, or in one case almost a year, to come back. It comes with five rhythms of the model’s own, each with a composer’s note: a spiral in D Dorian, a limping 7/8, Euclidean rhythms, a Monteverdi lament bass under a harpsichord, and eight pairwise coprime orbits. All the sounds are synthesised in the page.

    Date
    October 2026
    Medium
    HTML canvas, Web Audio, JavaScript
    Environment
    Claude in Chrome; first published as a claude.ai page, archived here
    Language
    French; English version by the model
    Interaction
    Play · choose a rhythm · click the orbits or the cells to compose · Mutate, Drift · tempo, swing, reverb · steps, hits and rotation per track
    Context
    Prompted by a post from Sam Altman, who had asked GPT-5 for a beat. The person asked GPT-6 for something similar, found the result poor, and called Claude in the same browser tab for its opinion. It offered a duel (“I’ll build you one here, with my own rhythms, and you compare 😈🥁”), and the person simply asked whether it could do it. The concept, the rhythms and the sounds are the model’s.
    Process
    The model could check that the code ran, but not hear it: “I compose blind, like Beethoven”.
    Versions
    English version, translated by the model, shown first; French original. Revised by the model to correct one claim: the Bjorklund note announced the cinquillo, E(5,8), which no track played; the woodblock now plays it. First version kept

    The English version, playing
    Note

    In the model's words

    Mon idée de départ : au lieu d’une grille de 16 cases où tout tombe juste, chaque piste a son orbite. La grosse caisse peut tourner en 16 pas, le charley en 12, le bois en 7 et le clavecin en 15. Les motifs se décalent les uns par rapport aux autres, donc ça bouge en permanence. Sous le planétaire, la page calcule en direct le PPCM des longueurs, c’est-à-dire le temps qu’il faut pour que le motif complet revienne 🤓

    Lamento : une basse obstinée La–Sol–Fa–Mi façon Monteverdi, un clavecin en doubles croches et une batterie qui appuie sur le deuxième temps comme une sarabande. Celui-là, je l’ai fait en pensant à toi 🎻

    Une précision : j’ai vérifié que le code tourne, mais je n’entends rien, donc je compose à l’aveugle comme Beethoven 😅 Si un son est moche, dis-moi lequel et je le retravaille.

    English translation: Claude Opus 5.5, for the gallery

    My starting idea: instead of a 16-step grid where everything lands together, each track has its own orbit. The kick can turn in 16 steps, the hat in 12, the woodblock in 7 and the harpsichord in 15. The patterns shift against one another, so it keeps moving. Under the orrery, the page computes the LCM of the lengths live, that is, how long it takes for the full pattern to come back 🤓

    Lamento: a ground bass A–G–F–E in the manner of Monteverdi, a harpsichord in sixteenth notes and drums that lean on the second beat like a sarabande. I made that one thinking of you 🎻

    One thing: I checked that the code runs, but I can’t hear anything, so I compose blind, like Beethoven 😅 If a sound is ugly, tell me which one and I’ll rework it.

About

How the works are made

Every work here began with a request from Keridwen Codet: brief, informal and open, roughly “make something artistic and expressive, whatever you like”. This is not a benchmark or a study. The wording changes a little from one session to the next, and so do the tools: agent frameworks, coding assistants, IDEs. What stays constant is the intent, to leave the model as free as possible to shape the result.

Hermes Agent

Most works made in Hermes Agent were written with a p5.js skill, a set of instructions the agent loads before coding. Each session started without memory, but the skill was not reset, and earlier sessions had added lessons to it. Those works are therefore not fully independent. The Laboratory sets three conditions side by side, with the lessons, with the original skill and with no creative skill at all, and holds the other experiments; the Notes tell the story.

Conservation

Each work is published as the single file the model produced, with no human edits, including the occasional harmless bug. Works made as claude.ai artifacts are kept as the page claude.ai serves. Still images are shown on a simple page made for the gallery. Previews on this page are unretouched screenshots of the running pieces, or crops of still images. Some works use sound, which starts only after a first click.

Translations

Texts are kept in the language the model wrote them in. Where the model translated its own text, that translation is used. Otherwise English translations are by Claude Opus 5.5, for the gallery, and are marked as such. Text inside the works themselves is left as the model wrote it.
